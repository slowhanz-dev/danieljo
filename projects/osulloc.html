The brief was to make tea approachable. The result was a set that doesn't ask you to understand ceramics — it asks you to sit down. Balanced form from every angle, at every price point.

Designed to be held

Tea has a ritual. The object that carries it must understand that ritual without performing it. The Osulloc tea ware begins where most ceramics end — at the moment someone picks it up for the first time and doesn't think about the weight.

Every handle was prototyped until the grip required no adjustment. The spout was tested at three different pouring angles. The lid seat was refined to eliminate the half-turn that plagues most teapots.

01

Celadon as a choice

The glaze is celadon — the pale blue-green of Korean ceramic tradition. Not applied for aesthetics, but because celadon responds to light the way tea itself does: differently at different times of day, always quietly.

A set that works as a whole

Cup, teapot, tray, pitcher, strainer. Each piece was designed separately, then tested together. The visual weight of the full set is intentional — neither dominant nor apologetic. It simply belongs on the table.

02

Accessible at every scale

The set was designed to work at every price point — which meant the proportions had to survive smaller production scales without losing the form. The same teapot reads correctly at the flagship store and at the local counter.
