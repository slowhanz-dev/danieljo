Every corporate dining space looks the same. This one doesn't. Reinterpreting Joseon Baekja through Naver's 'Connect One' identity — eight vessel forms that feel traditional and entirely contemporary at once.

A brand language in ceramic form

The brief arrived simply: Naver needed tableware for its new training center in Chuncheon. The unstated requirement was that it had to feel like Naver without saying so — the same quiet confidence the brand projects digitally, now translated into something you hold.

The starting point was Joseon Baekja — the white porcelain tradition that defines Korean ceramics at its most restrained. The form language of 'Connect One' gave the direction: connection, continuity, the circle completing itself.

01

Eight forms, one system

The system comprises eight vessel forms — cup, mug, small plate, medium plate, bowl, side dish, teapot, and water pitcher. Proportions were iterated at 0.5mm intervals until every piece read as part of the same family.

White that earns its silence

The glaze is clear over white porcelain — not a choice made by absence of decision, but by understanding that Baekja white is not blank. It carries light differently at morning and evening. The training center kitchen operates both.

02

Production

All eight forms were produced in-house at Betterhand Studio, from 3D model to final kiln firing. Tooling was developed for production volumes appropriate to the training center's daily capacity.
